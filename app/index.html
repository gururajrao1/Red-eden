Mars is poisoned. Could life survive it?

Martian soil is laced with perchlorate, baked by UV under a thin sky, and mostly dry. Yet Earth life eats poison, hides from the sun, and may once have started from chemistry alone. RED EDEN is one application for asking that question four ways: simulate it, play it, mix materials by hand, and read the science behind it.

Laboratory open →

Run a live agent-based evolution sim in your browser: seeded colonies or life from chemistry, tunable mutation, gene transfer, UV and poison. Watch traits fix in real time.

Garden game play →

A terraforming sandbox: paint soil, ice, germs and moss, scrub poison, raise UV domes. Solve 4 problems, unlock the field guide, turn red dirt green.

Library read →

46 papers behind every number: perchlorate on Mars, UV survival, wet-dry abiogenesis, biosignatures — each tied to something you can try, and 8 papers rewritten as fun 4–5 page stories by the project's reader agent.

Materials sandbox mix →

Sandboxels-style play across 10 worlds — Earth, Moon, Mars, Europa, Archaean, Titan, Io, Hadean, Venus, Enceladus: 130+ paintable materials, 30 lifeforms, 59 world problems with guided solutions, a pinned inspector plus rotating field clues, visible reactions everywhere.

How the three organs connect

The lab runs the same equations as our Python model (P(death) = 1 − e^(−Stress/Resistance), 28-locus genomes, HGT, protocell emergence). The game puts those ideas in your hands — poison-eaters, domes, succession. The new sandbox is the free bench: 130+ materials across 10 worlds, where every reaction is visible and every world changes the rules. The library shows which parts are established science and which are our hypotheses, with honest uncertainty labels. Nothing here claims Mars has life; everything here shows what life would have to do.
