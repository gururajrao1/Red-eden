What question is this answering?

Mars soil is poisonous and sun-blasted. So: could living things survive there — and how would they change? This page is a sandbox for that question. A computer grows thousands of virtual microbes under Mars-like stress and watches who lives, who dies, and which new tricks (like eating the poison) take over the colony.

Why it matters: farming Mars soil · choosing where rovers should look for life · judging whether Earth germs could contaminate Mars · testing how life itself might begin.

How to read this page in 30 seconds: 1. Watch the colony movie below. 2. Read “What this run taught us”. 3. Drag the time slider up top to travel back through history.

What this run taught us

Life survived here.
Population went from 300 to 1203. Under Mars-like poison and sun, the colony didn't just hang on — it spread.
The poison became food.
Perchlorate-eaters rose from 49% to 59% of the colony. Natural selection visibly favored microbes that digest the toxin and clean their surroundings.
Variety rose.
Genetic diversity went from 5.7 to 7.0. Mutation plus gene-sharing between neighbours kept the gene pool stocked — the raw material for future adaptation.
The colony got healthier over time.
Average health climbed from 0.56 to 0.63 — the signature of adaptation, not luck.
